General Information

Discover Seoul, Korea

Discover Seoul, KoreaGeneral InformationVisa Information

Korea

The Korean peninsula stretches southward from the centre of the north-east coast of Asia, with a land area of about 220,000 km² and some 3,400 islands. South Korea has a population of 50 million, of whom about 10 million live in the capital, Seoul. Over 5,000 years of history, Koreans have developed a distinctive culture; an exceptional respect for learning and education runs through the country's life.

Hangeul

The Korean alphabet, created in the 15th century under King Sejong, is one of the few writing systems whose origin is precisely documented. It is widely regarded as the most significant invention in Korean history.

Hansik

Korean food is built on fermentation: kimchi, soy sauce, soybean paste and red pepper paste. The government, industry and civic groups are working together to bring Hansik to the world.

Hanbok & Hanok

Hanbok is the traditional costume, worn today on festive days and special occasions. Hanok, the traditional house with its tile or thatched roof, still shapes neighbourhoods such as Bukchon in central Seoul.

Seoul

Seoul has been the capital of Korea for about 600 years, since the Joseon Dynasty. The Han River divides the city: the northern part is the centre of culture and history, the southern part the business district. The city has hosted the 1986 Asian Games, the 1988 Olympic Games and the 2002 FIFA World Cup, and today combines royal palaces and temples with a modern skyline.

Attractions

  • Art — over 700 museums and 400 galleries, including the National Museum of Korea
  • Tradition — Gyeongbokgung Palace, Bukchon Hanok Village
  • Culture — performances such as NANTA and JUMP
  • Modern — Daehakro, Hongdae and the Hangang riverside parks
  • UNESCO World Heritage — Jongmyo Shrine, Changdeokgung Palace, the Royal Tombs of the Joseon Dynasty
